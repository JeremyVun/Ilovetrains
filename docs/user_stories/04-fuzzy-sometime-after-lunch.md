# Sometime after lunch

**Persona.** Sam, works from home most days, goes into the office once or
twice a week at no fixed time. Saved trip: Epping → Central.

**Situation.** 13:10, finishing lunch. Sam will leave "in a bit", maybe
forty minutes, maybe an hour. Not stressed. Wants a rough picture of the
afternoon frequency to plan around a phone call.

**Goal.** Fuzzy: "how often do trains go this time of day, so I can pick a
call slot and still get in by three-ish?"

## What happens

1. Sam opens the app. The header shows the next train, which is irrelevant.
2. Sam taps the trip row and reads the board as a timetable: six departures
   and their arrivals.
3. Sam decides "trains every 15 minutes, I'll aim for the one around 14:10"
   and closes the app.

## Success looks like

- The board reads as a frequency at a glance: regular gaps, clock times
  dominant, countdowns secondary.
- Sam's mental note ("the 14:10") is something the app will help with later
  without Sam having to find it again.

## Pressure points

- The board is anchored at *now* and stops six services out. For a plan an
  hour ahead it may simply not reach.
- The countdown figures (45px, primary ink) dominate the row, but for this
  goal the clock times are the answer. Does the row's hierarchy fight a
  planner's reading?
- Sam cannot mark "the 14:10" without focusing it, and focusing turns the
  home screen into directions for a train Sam is not on for the next hour.
  Is there a lighter way to remember a chosen train?
- Nothing on the board says how far into the future it reaches, so "these
  six are all there is" and "there are more later" look the same.
