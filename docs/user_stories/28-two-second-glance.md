# The two-second glance

**Persona.** Anyone, any day.

**Situation.** Walking, phone raised for two seconds, screen at half
brightness, thumb over the bottom of the screen.

**Goal.** Fuzzy: "am I okay?"

## What happens

1. The eye lands on the largest thing: the figure. Then the warning colour
   if there is any. Then the platform cap.
2. Everything else is not read.

## Success looks like

- The three things the glance catches are the three that matter: how long,
  is anything wrong, which platform.
- Nothing wrong is ever hidden in small type or colour alone.

## Pressure points

- The header block is 284px from the top of the screen to the rule, with
  about 34px of slack above and below the content. On a short Android frame
  the trip list starts below the middle of the screen. Is the header's air
  earning its place, or pushing the second most useful thing down?
- Delay is carried by the figure's colour and a 10px word. Cancellation is
  carried by a dash and an 11px uppercase sentence. At two seconds and half
  brightness, is a coral `9` distinguishable from a white one?
- `RUNNING` in the top line is the same size and colour as `NEXT TRAIN`. A
  glance cannot tell "you are following a train" from "here is a train".
- The receipt, when present, is a 15px sentence below the instruction. It
  is the one line that explains a leap and the last thing the eye reaches.
