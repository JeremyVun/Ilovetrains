Final Detail alignment and rounded joins

Problem The native Detail freshness label sat below the journey, Android step clocks aligned left, and a ground-colour spacer cut a dark slit between the red ride and its alighting marker. The same spacer hid a blue overhang by flattening the boarding marker’s left edge.

Fix Freshness now shares the top row with Back, Android step clocks align to the trailing edge, and all three clients use measured chip geometry. Ride paint overlaps inside the chip by its corner radius while timetable anchors and dwell gaps stay unchanged.

User impact Source status is visible before the journey heading, times scan as one column, the red route joins its marker continuously, and the next blue marker keeps its rounded left corners without a stray blue segment.

D1–D3 and D8 — route geometry

Problem Android grouped transfer labels across stops, repeated the later alighting pin, warned on a cancelled tight change, and exposed blue track before a blue marker. Both native clients also inherited the artificial ground slit before an alighting marker.

Fix Stops, dwell labels, pins, cancellation treatment, and coloured segment endpoints follow one measured layout. Ride paint meets the alighting chip and starts inside the boarding chip’s rounded body; actual transfer dwell gaps remain on their original time coordinates.

User impact Each change reads in travel order and platform colour no longer implies an extra segment.

D5 — Now and hour typography

Problem Native figure sizing diverged for the word “Now” and wide hour tokens.

Fix Both native clients use compact figures for Now and wide tokens, while H/min remain separate small units.

User impact Immediate and multi-hour departures fit without crowding the platform strip or times.

D4 — countdowns across source states

Problem Web and native clients could hide future countdowns when a board was offline, stale, retained, or schedule-only, while native past rows could also lose their elapsed number.

Fix Every future source state keeps Now, minutes, or rounded hours. Past rows show a rounded elapsed number plus “Ago”; 119 minutes rounds to 2H.

User impact Riders retain the time estimate while source labels still explain whether it is scheduled, live, stale, or offline.

C4 and C8 — recent degraded source

Problem A fresh board marked degraded could hide the Next countdown and Running late state even though web keeps both.

Fix Fresh degraded data retains its figure and delay status. All source states keep countdowns; schedule-only source still does not claim observed lateness.

User impact A warning dot no longer removes still-current departure guidance.

C3 — full ferry caps

Problem Native ferry stops could collapse numbered/side wharf labels.

Fix Initial boarding caps keep the full wharf number and side; transfer chips keep compact grammar.

User impact Ferry riders retain the side needed to board the correct service.

C9 and F6 — saved-trip receipts

Problem Native clients omitted the one-open Just added receipt and could invent a line badge for trips with no known lines.

Fix Predicted location-created trips show Just added; empty-line trips show names and arrow without a fabricated badge or spine.

User impact A new automatic trip is explainable, and unknown data no longer appears certain.

D6 — feedback focus and draft retention

Problem Android lacked the focused field treatment and screen-local draft state could be lost during navigation.

Fix Both native forms keep model-owned draft/category state and show the active message field. No feedback request was sent.

User impact Leaving the form briefly does not erase a report in progress.
