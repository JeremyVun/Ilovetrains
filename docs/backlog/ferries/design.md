# Ferries

what satisfies them. The item is ready to build when "Open questions" is
empty and `build_plan.md` exists.

## What it is

Sydney Ferries become a mode the app plans, beside trains and metro. A
wharf is a station: it can be searched, saved as either end of a trip,
recognised as where the user is standing, and inferred as home. A journey
may be ferry only, or trains and ferries with a change between them. The
board, the smart header, journey detail and focused directions render a
ferry leg with the same devices a train leg uses, in one ferry green, and
the copy says `ferry` and `Wharf` where a train journey says `train` and
`Platform`.

Why it earns its place: the harbour is a real commute (Manly, Mosman,
Parramatta River) and most of it is train-less, so today those riders have
nothing. Ferries are timetabled and realtime-tracked like trains; nothing
about the board's honesty rules, the connection policy or the prediction
heuristic has to change in kind. Buses are deliberately not this item.

## Verified premises

Design baseline checked 2026-09-05 before implementation. Current upstream
observations and captured shapes are in `docs/references/tfnsw-open-data.md`.

- Upstream sends ferries as EFA product class 9. The server currently asks
  upstream to exclude classes 4, 5, 7, 9, 10 and 11 (`internal/tfnsw/
  client.go`), drops any journey with a service leg outside classes 1 and 2
  (`serviceLegs` in `map.go`) and reports `modes` for classes 1 and 2 only
  (the stops index). The originally named `serveableModes` helper did not
  exist in this checkout.
- `/api/v1/stops` is answered from a baked index generated by
  `tools/build-stations.js` from three GTFS bundles, keeping
  `location_type = 1` rows served by a rail `route_type`. The same file is
  `web/stations.json`, in the service-worker `SHELL`, and it is what
  smart-header-v2's nearest-station and home inference read. GTFS
  parent-station ids equal Trip Planner stop ids for rail (verified
  2026-09-05, `docs/references/tfnsw-open-data.md`, "Station index").
- Line colour on the client is keyed by `line.name` alone (`lineColour`,
  `lineFill` in `web/js/lines.js`); every filled device and bare text use
  goes through those two functions. `web/test/theme.test.js` requires every
  key in `COLOURS` to have `--line-<key>` in both schemes at 2.2:1 dark and
  4.5:1 light as text, and paper on `--line-fill-<key>` at 3:1 in light.
- `platformNumber` in `web/js/journey.js` strips only a leading `Platform `.
  The literal words `Platform` and `train` appear in eight client strings
  (listed under "Copy").
- The connection floor (`connectionFloorMet`) and ceiling
  (`longestConnection`) measure scheduled arrival of one service leg to
  scheduled departure of the next. Walking legs (class 99) are folded away
  first, but they carry `origin.departureTimePlanned`,
  `destination.arrivalTimePlanned` and `duration` (240 s at Strathfield in
  `trip_rhodes_bondijunction.json`), so the walk inside a gap is
  measurable.
- Official line colours: TfNSW publishes ten Sydney Ferries colours, F1
  Manly `#00774B` through F10 Blackwattle Bay `#5AB031`, on the Open Data
  Hub resources page. Contrast measured 2026-09-05: as bare text F2 fails
  the dark floor (1.86:1) and F3, F4, F6, F7, F9, F10 fail the light floor;
  as light fills F4 (1.54:1), F6, F7, F9 and F10 fail 3:1.

## Rulings

1. **Ferry endpoints use verified Trip Planner stops** (revised by owner,
   2026-09-05: “hrm ok makes sense proceed”). Sydney Ferries GTFS determines
   which wharves are served; a captured, validated mapping supplies their
   Trip Planner stop IDs. Group boarding wharves under that stop. Where a
   stop already exists in the rail index, merge modes: Circular Quay is one
   `Circular Quay` entry with `train · ferry`, ID `200020`. Manly is
   `Manly Wharf`, ID `209573`. Journey legs specify the actual boarding
   platform or wharf, including its side. This supersedes separate station
   and wharf entries where upstream uses one hub. Private-only wharves
   remain outside the index.
2. **Vehicle and place words follow the first leg's mode** (2026-09-05).
   `train`/`Platform` for train and metro, `ferry`/`Wharf` for a ferry
   leg, substituted into the existing strings. With no journey known yet
   the generic `train` stays (the metro ruling of the same day: `train` is
   the generic word). Rejected: `train` everywhere with only the place
   word fixed; a mode-neutral sweep (`Next service`), blander and a larger
   blast radius against the exemplars.
3. **The change floor measures slack: gap minus any walking leg**
   (2026-09-05). When upstream places a walking leg between two services,
   its duration is subtracted before the floor and ceiling are judged.
   With no walking leg the gap is unchanged, so every existing rail board
   keeps its rows. Rejected: keeping the walk inside the gap (a 1-minute
   spare passes as 5); a second, larger floor for mode changes (a knob the
   principles forbid, and still walk-inclusive).
4. **One shared ferry green for every ferry line** (2026-09-05). Ten codes,
   one colour, keyed by mode rather than by code, so a private operator's
   ferry (class 9 with an unknown code) paints the same green. Rejected:
   darkening each of the ten to clear 3:1 (ten light-scheme derivations
   for lines nobody tells apart by colour); T1-style exceptions (F4 lime
   at 1.5:1 is unreadable, so it was never really an exception for all
   five).
5. **Nearest-station ties within 200 m go to a saved-trip end, then the
   nearest** (2026-09-05). This applies when distinct indexed stops fall within the same radius;
   Circular Quay is now one hub under revised ruling 1. Rejected: always
   the nearest (can offer a wharf trip to a nearby train commuter);
   rail over wharf (wrong for the Manly commuter).

## Mechanism

### 1. Index: the ferries bundle

`tools/build-stations.js` gains the Sydney Ferries bundle at
`https://api.transport.nsw.gov.au/v1/gtfs/schedule/ferries/sydneyferries`
(mode `ferry`, `MODE_ORDER` `train, metro, ferry`). Join ferry route types
(4, 1000–1099 or 1200) through trips and stop times to served boarding
stops, then resolve each through a committed mapping of GTFS stop IDs to
verified Trip Planner stops. The mapping is captured by explicit upstream
probes and includes canonical IDs, names and coordinates; generation from
`--from-dir` stays offline and fails on an unmapped served boarding stop.
`--from-dir` expects `ferries.zip` beside the other three bundles.

Duplicate Trip Planner IDs merge their modes. Ferry-only names end in
`Wharf`; a shared rail/ferry hub loses the rail-only `Station` suffix.
Fuzzy ranking strips `wharf` as it does `station`, on both client and
server. The generator reports names ending in neither suffix, including
intentional shared hubs. Regeneration bumps service-worker `VERSION`.

### 2. Server: class 9 is served

Three lists become one fact. `modeName` maps class 9 to `"ferry"`; the
upstream exclusions drop `exclMOT_9`; `serviceLegs` accepts class 9;
the baked stop index publishes its mode. `mode` is `"train"`, `"metro"` or `"ferry"` on
journeys, legs and stops. Nothing else in the mapping is mode-aware: a
wharf's `platformName` (including `Wharf 3, Side A`, `Side A` or a named
wharf in captured responses) travels in
`platform` unchanged, realtime is gated per leg as today, cancellation
detection is the same loose match.

Connection policy measures slack. When walking legs (classes 99 and 100) sit between
two service legs, the sum of their durations is subtracted from the gap
between the earlier service's scheduled arrival and the later service's
scheduled departure before the floor and the ceiling are applied. A gap
with no walking leg is unchanged. The upstream `duration` is used when
present, else the walking leg's own planned arrival minus departure.
Leading or trailing walks that move boarding/alighting to another stop
are omitted: the existing response has no outer-walk instruction, so keeping
them would mislabel the departure or destination. Walks within the same
endpoint hub remain valid. A change across distinct stops names both stops
in detail, while focused alighting names the previous service's stop and
boarding names the next service's stop.

Contract text in `api.md` records the rule and that the measured value is
never rewritten into the response: the client still derives transfer wait
from the leg times it is given.

### 3. Client: colour by mode, words by mode

`web/js/lines.js` gains a colour key: a line whose mode is `ferry` paints
`FERRY` whatever its code; every other line paints its code. `COLOURS`
gains `FERRY: '#5AB031'` (the official F10/Stockton green, which is also
TfNSW's mode green), `web/app.css` gains `--line-FERRY` and
`--line-fill-FERRY` in the dark scheme and a darkened `--line-FERRY` in
light, and the theme test covers it like any key. The visible code text
(`Board F1 · Manly`) stays the code.

Light-scheme arithmetic: `#5AB031` is 2.59:1 on paper `#FAF9F5`, so it is
darkened on its own hue until it clears 4.5:1 as text. `#428024` measures
4.59:1 as text and, being the fill too, 4.59:1 under paper numerals, so no
fill exception is needed. A builder re-measures with the theme test's own
functions and records the ratio in the CSS comment as the other lines do.

Every call that colours a device by `leg.code` or `row.lineCode` passes the
colour key instead: `rowmodel.js` (row colour and fill), `journey.js`
(chips and leg specs), `journeybar.js` (segments, caps, pins, stems),
`home.js` (saved-trip badges), `detail.js` (chips). The key is derived
once, where the leg is read, so no renderer looks at `mode`.

Words: a `modeWords(mode)` helper returns `{vehicle, place}`:
`{train, Platform}` for `train` and `metro`, `{ferry, Wharf}` for `ferry`.
`platformNumber` extracts a real platform or wharf number; side-only or
named wharves use a dash in compact chips and retain their full upstream
labels in directions and accessibility text. The strings in
"Copy" take their word from the first leg of the journey they describe;
a change step's place word follows the leg being boarded. Loading and
no-journey states keep the generic `train`.

Setup sheet: the mode label already prints `modes` joined by ` · `, so
Manly reads `ferry` and Circular Quay reads `train · ferry`. No new device.

### 4. Nearest-station tie-break

The shared `stations.js` lookup helper supplies smart-header-v2's
`here` tiers. Tier 1 picks any index stop within 200 m, preferring a saved
trip end when several qualify, then the nearest. The ferry build adopts
this module from smart-header-v2 and adds the saved-stop tie-break. The
smart-header controller subsequently landed on main and now uses these
tiers for location-based selection. Shared hubs such as Circular Quay have no station
versus wharf tie; separate nearby stops still use the rule.

### 5. Journey identity

Mixed F1 and MFF itineraries can share the same first train departure.
The client identifies an itinerary by the ordered `(line.name,
departure.scheduled)` pairs of all service legs. This governs row selection
and focused-journey refresh matching; the persisted focus already stores the
whole journey, so no storage migration is required.

### 6. Verification instruments

`tools/probe-tfnsw.sh` gains a ferry probe set (Circular Quay wharf to
Manly; Wynyard Station to Manly for a train-to-ferry change) whose
fixtures the Go golden tests read. `tools/shoot-states.js` gains a saved
ferry trip and a train-plus-ferry journey so the board, home, detail and
focused states are shot with a wharf cap, a green segment and the ferry
words, at both schemes and the standard frames. Exemplars in
`assets/comps/latest/` for any screen whose pixels change are replaced
from those shots.

(`gpt-5.6-sol`, 2026-09-05) and recorded verbatim. Its one concern, that
the loading string must stay generic because no journey is known yet, is
already the rule. **Owner verdict 2026-09-05: all nine approved as
written.** Phase 3 may write these strings and no others.

| Home top line, distance unknown | `Next train` | `Next ferry` |

| Journey detail focus button | `Take this train` | `Take this ferry` |
| Cancelled row lead note | `<time> cancelled · next train` | `<time> cancelled · next ferry` |

Quay, following revised ruling 1.

## Edge cases

- **Private ferries.** Manly Fast Ferry and other class-9 operators may
  appear in journeys. They are served, painted ferry green, and their
  code prints as upstream gives it. Their wharves are not in the index
  unless shared with Sydney Ferries.
- **Wharf with no number.** Named wharves and side-only labels remain
  visible verbatim, with `—` in the compact chip. When upstream omits the
  platform entirely, the chip is `—` and the cap is omitted.
- **Change from ferry to ferry at Circular Quay.** Two wharves, one walk
  leg between them. Slack rule applies; chips are both green with wharf
  numerals, which is the reason ruling 4 accepted losing per-line colour.
- **Ferry then train at Circular Quay, walking leg absent.** If upstream
  ever omits the walk (it does for platform-to-platform rail changes) the
  gap is the whole signal, as today. Phase-0 fixtures settle whether it
  is present for station-to-wharf.
- **Short boards.** Ferry headways are 20 to 30 minutes off-peak. A board
  asking for 6 gets what upstream gives; the existing "short, not padded"
  rule holds and nothing is invented.
- **Past windows.** Actuals for ferries follow the same realtime ageing;
  nothing mode-specific.
- **Old clients.** A client from before this change receiving
  `mode: "ferry"` paints the fallback ink and prints `Platform` on a wharf
  number until it updates; the worker `VERSION` bump makes that one load.
- **Home inferred as a wharf.** Legitimate for a Manly resident; the
  reverse-trip and way-back logic is station-agnostic.
