Part 1 · correction 2  the gap, accurately to scale — at 4×

You asked for “great care for the head and tail to be correctly positioned so that the transfer line gap shows accurately scaled”. The old bar could not do it, and the reason was arithmetic rather than care: the two platform numerals were laid out as flex siblings, so they ate about 40px of the bar before a single minute was drawn, and the runs then shared what was left over. The ratio 27 : 7 : 10 was right; the positions were not — the end of leg 1 was drawn 24px away from where it happens, and the gap came out 30% narrow.

The bar is now a time axis: 0% is departure, 100% is arrival, and every mark on it is a percentage of the journey's own minutes. The numerals stopped displacing the runs and started overlaying them — the alight numeral's right edge pinned to the end of leg 1 (the tail of the red line), the boarding numeral's left edge to the start of leg 2 (the head of the blue one). What is left between them is the dwell, at its true share. No minimum gap width, as you ruled.

Deviation is 0.0px on every scenario at every width — 390, 412, 360, the board row, the home header at full measure, and the focus strip. It is checked by arithmetic rather than by eye: the drawing carries the minutes it claims to obey in the page itself, and measure.js recomputes the picture from them.

Part 2 · the marker, at 4×

You asked for “some nice progress icon on the line”. It is made of the two things this system already owns and nothing else: a notch cut through the filled bar in the page's own ground, with a 2px core of the line you are on, and a small filled wedge above it. A wedge over a rule is a printed index mark — a ruler's cursor. A circle on a route would be a map, which this is not.

The part of the journey behind you is dimmed, which is not a new idea either: it is your own ruling that past rows are future rows dimmed, applied at the scale of one journey. The marker is grey at rest at both ends and takes the line's colour in motion — in the dwell it wears the colour of the line it is about to board, because a directions object points forward.

The structural question · does the focus strip survive?

Both frames are 09:33: you are nine minutes into the 09:24 and you have the board open. Same data, same clock, one difference.

MY RECOMMENDATION: DELETE IT. The strip's original reason is written in the shipped code — “once you are on the train the only copy of it left is the one in localStorage”. Board v2 made that untrue. The board now keeps past departures, so your train is still there, above the NOW rule, dimmed. The strip's remaining content is the home header's content word for word, and the price is measured — though the row height correction has changed what the price is. It no longer costs you a service: rows are a sixth of whatever frame is left, so six still fit under the strip. It costs 21px out of every row on the board — 118.7 down to 98 at 390×844, and 100 down to 79 on the 412×732 Android, three pixels clear of the row's own ink and the tightest the board gets anywhere in this workshop.

What the board loses, honestly. It stops saying which departed service is yours — the 9min AGO row looks like any other row that has run — and it stops carrying the instruction and the marker while you are looking at it, which is one tap back to home. If you want the board to know, the cheapest honest mark is the tracked row's hairline promoted to the heavy rule the NOW anchor already uses. I have not comped that; say the word.

What I would like ruled

1. The strip. Delete, or keep and pay 21px of every row on the board for it.
2. 228px of empty page under three tight trip rows — accept, or give some of it back to the rows.

A PLATFORM 1 cap beside an instruction reading Platform 5 is the same ambiguity you complained about with the minutes figure, so the cap is printed until you board and then the bar takes the whole page.
