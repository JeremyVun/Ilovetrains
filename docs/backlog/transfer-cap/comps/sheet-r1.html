Transfer cap · round 1

The cap is settled: the server drops journeys with more than two changes, and a rider who wants them turns the cap off in Settings. Two things are not settled, and this sheet is for both. Where the new choice lives on the Settings screen, and what a board row looks like under Any, when a journey has three changes.

Every frame here is drawn by the app's own code. The Settings screens are the shipped renderer, so Personal, Services, Appearance and the version row are the real thing, not a redrawing. The two-change board reproduces the calibration exemplar pixel for pixel, which is how you can trust the three-change ones beside it.

No captured response anywhere in the repository holds a journey with three changes, so one had to be made: the Rhodes to Bondi Junction service keeps its real departure and its real Town Hall arrival, and is cut at Strathfield and at Central to add the two extra changes. The station names, platforms, lines and times are all real.

  • S1 No captured response in the repository holds a journey with three changes, so the Rhodes to Bondi Junction service is cut at Strathfield, a station its own line calls at, and at Central. Its real departure and its real Town Hall arrival are untouched.
  • S2 One board renames the three change stations to the three longest station names the app knows, at 20 and 21 characters, to size the name band against the widest value it can ever be given.
  • S3 One board applies S1 to all six services, which is the board an uncapped answer can legally return.
  • S4 The enlarged-text frames grow every font size by 1.3 and leave every box where it is, which is what an Android font scale of 1.3 does.
  • S5 The Settings states — the choice on its other value, a 29-character home station, every service off — are ordinary states of a control that is not built yet.

THE THINGS TO JUDGE.

(1) The Settings choice. Four shapes, three positions, two candidate word pairs, and one of them with a note line. My pick is the row inside Services: it is the only one that leaves the whole screen on one page at 412×732, costing 9px of scroll where the three banded versions cost 71px, 79px and 83px. Its risk is that the value sits on the left and the mark on the right, so you have to read both to know which one you are on.

(2) The journey line under Any. Today's row does not survive a third change, and the reason is the platform numerals, not the station names. My pick is c4's treatment: hide the alighting numeral at every change after the first, print the change names at the smaller of the two label sizes, and give a pin that still lands on its neighbour the smaller box the 360px phone already uses. It is the only one of the four where nothing is covered and nothing is out of order at 390, at 412 and at enlarged text.

The two questions are independent. Any of the four Settings shapes can ship with any of the four journey lines.
