Settings choice with the words **Up to 2 / Any number** and no note, and

The findings in section 3 outrank every concept here; two of them are defects
in code that already ships.

## 2 Ground rules held to

**The spec, verbatim, and where each item lands.**

1. *"cap the maximum number of transfers at 2, and add a setting so users can
   choose their own limit."* Every concept renders one new Settings control
   with exactly two values, on the approved Grouped-buttons screen.
2. *"add the default of 2, add the uncapped 'any' option in settings."* Every
   concept ships both values in both states: the `settings` scenario is the
   capped default, the `any` scenario is the uncapped one.
3. *"Correct placement does not make three or four names on one line
   readable."* Confirmed, and inverted: at three changes the names are the part
   that still works. Section 3, finding 4.

**Borrowed, not invented.** `product.js` is the shipped client's own modules —
`version, time, dom, lines, journey, journeybar, rowmodel, board, focus, home,
settings` and `web/test/fixture.js` — concatenated into one classic script with
only `import` lines and the word `export` removed, because modules are blocked
under `file://`. Every Settings screen on this sheet is `settings.js`'s own
`shell`, `personal`, `services`, `appearance` and `icon` output; every board is
`board.js`'s `boardHtml` over `rowmodel.js`'s `boardModel`; every journey line
is `journeybar.js`. Blob hashes at bundle time are in `product.js`'s header.
`base.css` is `new-round.js`'s verbatim copy of `web/app.css`. The round adds
30 lines of CSS, all in `cap-common.css`.

**Calibration.** `c1-pair-390x844-two.png` is **pixel-identical** to
`assets/comps/latest/board-390x844-two-change.png` —
`node tools/comps/diff.js` reports `0 / 1316640 px differ`. Every three-change
frame is the same code path with one more change in the data.

**Data.** `web/test/fixture.js`'s `transferBody()`: the six captured
Rhodes → Bondi Junction services from
`tools/fixtures/trip_rhodes_bondijunction.json`, to the second. Station ids and
names are `web/stations.json`'s. Copy is Codex's draft file verbatim
(`/tmp/transfer-cap-copy-r1.md`); nothing else is written on any control.

**Synthetic deltas, and there are no others** (also declared in `cap-data.js`'s
header and in the sheet's own "What is synthetic"):

- **S1 three changes.** `threeLegJourney()`'s edit — the T4 leg cut short at
  Central, a T1 leg run Central → Bondi Junction — generalised to any of the
  six corridor journeys, then the T9 leg cut at Strathfield, a station that
  line genuinely calls at between Rhodes and Town Hall, with a T1 running
  Strathfield → Town Hall. Applied to journey 1 the first half reproduces the
  repository's own `threeLegJourney()` to the millisecond (checked by
  comparison after parsing every timestamp). The captured Rhodes departure and
  Town Hall arrival are untouched, so the axis keeps the fixture's endpoints:
  9/5/13/7/4/5/15 minutes, 58 total.
- **S2 the widest legal names.** The three change stations renamed to
  Macquarie University, Newcastle Interchange and International Airport — the
  three longest names in `web/stations.json` at 20, 21 and 21 characters. No
  route joins them; the scenario sizes the name band against the widest value
  the vocabulary permits.
- **S3 every journey.** S1 applied to all six services: the board an uncapped
  answer can legally return.
- **S4 enlarged text.** `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 130%` on the root, which is
  Blink's own text-scale multiplier: every font size grows 1.3× and every px
  box stays where it is, exactly as an Android font scale of 1.3 acts on sp
  text in a dp layout. Measured in this harness: 14px → 18.2px, 10px → 13px.
- **S5 Settings states.** The choice on its non-default value; the
  29-character International Airport Station as the local home override; every
  service off. Ordinary states of a control that is not built yet.

**Frozen, and kept.** The Grouped-buttons composition (Personal rows, four
equal service buttons, three equal appearance choices, quiet feedback and
version rows, 44px targets, visible non-colour marks): unchanged in all four,
and every frame's smallest tap target measures 44px. Every change still names
its station beneath its own dwell midpoint. No copy is ellipsised anywhere.
Two values only, capped by default. The `off` scenario proves the screen with
the flag off. `web/app.css` was not forked.

**What is NOT proven here.** Persistence, the flags fetch, refetch on change,
the offline planner, Android and iOS. These are comps.

## 3 Findings that outrank the concepts

1. **A three-change row draws two platform numerals in the wrong order and on
   top of each other, today.** At 390×844 the Central alighting pin (12) is
   drawn 43px over the Town Hall boarding pin (5) and to its LEFT, so the row
   reads "12 then 5" for a journey that boards at 5 and alights at 12. At
   412×732 the overlap is 41.4px. Cause: the Town Hall → Central leg is 4 of
   the journey's 58 minutes, so the leg is ~11px wide on a 156px axis while the
   two pins that bracket it need ~55px. The 3px ground-colour separator
   `ui.md` requires is covered. This is not a comp defect — it is
   `journeybar.js` as it ships, and the same board would show it the day the
   server returns a three-change journey with the cap off.
   Frames: `zoom-pins-c1-pair`, `c1-pair-390x844-three`.
2. **The Home smart header breaks harder than the board.** The
   "second change's alighting pin stays hidden" rule is a `.sy-row.two` class
   rule; the header has no such class, so it draws all six numerals. On a
   227.8px header axis they cover 80%, with the same inversion and 48.7px of
   overlap. The header is also inconsistent between frames: the same journey
   stacks its three names on three lines at 390 and on two at 412, because the
   product's placement is opportunistic (each name drops a line only if it hits
   one already placed). Frame: `zoom-home-c1-pair`.
3. **At enlarged text nothing fits without giving something up.** At 390 and
   font scale 1.3 the six pins want 115% of the axis they sit on; two numerals
   are cut into and the product's own clamp fires (`CLAMPED sy-bar 1` in
   `shots/report.json`). Shooting the two-change board at the same scale
   produces no clamp, so the third change causes it, not the text size.
   The only treatment with zero overlap at 390/1.3 is c4's, and it needs all
   three of its remedies to get there. Frames: `zoom-big-c1-pair`,
   `zoom-big-c4-lines`.
4. **Station-name length is not the problem.** Replacing the three names with
   the three longest in the app (S2) changes nothing measurable: same three
   lines, same 60px band, same row height, none shortened or cut, at both
   frames and both schemes. The owner's "three or four names on one line" is
   right about the outcome and the mechanism is the pins.
   Frames: `c1-pair-390x844-three-long-light` beside `c1-pair-390x844-three`.
5. **The Settings screen's "one page at 412×732" property is spent by any new
   group.** Measured scroll extent at 412×732: today **0px**; c2's row **9px**;
   c1's band **71px**; c4's band **79px**; c3's band and note **83px**. At
   390×844 all five are 0px. This was settings-02's headline finding for the
   approved composition, so it is the owner's to spend.
6. **A three-change row costs the board a service.** The row grows 127px →
   145px (the name band 42px → 60px). At 390 a board whose lead row has three
   changes shows five whole services instead of six; at 412, four instead of
   five. With all six services at three changes (S3) it is four at 390 and
   three at 412. c4's tighter band gets the row to 135px, which restores the
   sixth service at 390 and the fifth on the all-six board.
7. **Not this round's, but seen:** at font scale 1.3 the provenance word under
   the figure (`SCHEDULED`) overflows its box by 13px on every board frame,
   including the two-change one. It is a pre-existing web-client property at
   enlarged text.

## 4 The concepts

Each concept is one answer to question 1 and one answer to question 2. The two
are independent: any Settings shape can ship with any journey line.

### c1 · the pair — the literal reading, and the before for question 2

The Appearance band with two choices instead of three, in its own group between
Services and Appearance, no note. Emotional target: the screen has three
families of choice and this is plainly one of them.

- **Where the spec lands:** own `Changes` group after Services; values
  `Up to 2` / `Any number`; selected carries both the check and the 2px
  underline; radio semantics with Codex's accessible phrase.
- **Motion:** immediate state replacement, as Appearance.
- **Build cost:** **S** — one more `st-choice-set` and a two-column grid rule.
- **Why this might be wrong:** 96px of screen and 71px of scroll at 412 for a
  choice with two short words in it, and the check mark ends up 65px from the
  label it marks because the cell is half the page wide.
- **Passes:** the band began at the Appearance 86px and came down to 64px once
  it was clear a choice with no preview does not need preview height. Its
  journey line is deliberately left untouched, so this strip doubles as the
  before.

### c2 · the row — the subtraction, recommended for question 1

The Location row's own composition without the icon column: title on the left,
the current value in the subtitle, the other value in the mark on the right. It
joins the Services group under the services note, so the screen gains no new
heading. Emotional target: nothing was added, a line was written.

- **Where the spec lands:** inside `Services`, after the note; values
  `Up to 2` / `Any number`, one shown and one offered; one 56px button read as
  title, subtitle, action, exactly as `ui.md` specifies for Location.
- **Motion:** the value and the mark swap on tap.
- **Build cost:** **S** — one row, and the same two-column row shape Settings
  already has minus its icon column.
- **Why this might be wrong:** the mark names the value you are NOT on, which
  is the Location row's grammar (`TURN OFF` when it is on) but reads as a
  statement rather than an action when both sides are values. There is also no
  glyph for it in the product's icon set, so it is the one row on the screen
  with an empty icon column.
- **Passes:** the row first sat between the four buttons and the services note,
  which made the note — and, worse, the red all-off warning — read as this
  row's explanation. Moving it below the note fixed both; the `all-off` frame
  is the proof.
- **Its journey line:** every numeral kept. Pins are walked in travel order and
  slid until each has its separator back, by at most 22px at 390 and 30px at
  412, 41px on the Home header. Nothing is hidden and the order is right; the
  price is that the right two-thirds of the line becomes a block of chips with
  no visible time in it, and at 390/1.3 even packing leaves 19.4px of overlap.

### c3 · the note — what a note and the other words cost

The compact band, 48px, with a note line beneath it in the services-note idiom,
placed last, after Appearance, on the argument that a rider sets it once. It
carries the other candidate pair so both are on the sheet.

- **Where the spec lands:** own `Changes` group after Appearance; values
  `2 max` / `No limit`; note `Trips stay within the chosen change limit.`
- **Build cost:** **S**.
- **Why this might be wrong:** the note says nothing the two values have not
  said, which is the round's copy question in the flesh; and the group after
  Appearance reads as an afterthought, with its note sitting directly above the
  feedback rows where it can be mistaken for their caption.
- **Passes:** one, to bring the band down to 48px so the group with a note is
  not taller than a band without one by more than the note itself (108px
  against 96px).
- **Its journey line:** the existing two-change rule generalised — the
  alighting numeral is hidden at every change after the first, not only the
  second. Four numerals instead of six, no overlap, no inversion, pin cover
  down from 83% to 63% at 390. The Central arrival platform is the fact given
  up.

### c4 · the line — braver, recommended for question 2

The values are drawn, not only named: each choice carries a real journey line
as its preview, two changes on the left and more on the right, rendered by the
same code that draws the board. Appearance shows you the two palettes rather
than describing them; this shows you the two journeys. No note line, because
the picture is the note. Emotional target: you can see what you are buying.

- **Where the spec lands:** own `Changes` group after Services; values
  `Up to 2` / `Any number` under a 92px specimen; check and underline as
  Appearance.
- **Motion:** immediate; the specimens are static.
- **Build cost:** **M** — the only concept that needs a journey to render
  inside Settings, which means a real journey to render, and a decision about
  which one.
- **Why this might be wrong:** the uncapped specimen shows exactly three
  changes, which is a picture of one journey standing for "any number"; a
  rider could read it as a promise. It also paints line colours for services
  the rider may have turned off, which the `all-off` frame shows. And it is
  118px of screen — 79px of scroll at 412 — for two words.
- **Passes:** the band started at Appearance's 86px and came down to 72px; the
  specimen dropped its pins, boarding cap and names to become a bar of segments
  rather than a small unreadable row.
- **Its journey line:** all three remedies together — c3's hidden alighting
  numerals, the change names at 9px with a 3px lead instead of 10px with 6px,
  and the 360px phone's own smaller pin box on any three-change row, with the
  travel-order slide as a backstop. Result at every frame and at font scale
  1.3: **zero overlap, zero inversions**, at most 4px of pin movement off true.
  The row is 135px rather than 145px, which is the sixth service back at 390.

## 5 Vocabulary and contract additions needed

Each is an owner call.

1. **The group name and values.** `Changes`, with `Up to 2` and `Any number`
   (recommended) or `2 max` and `No limit`. Whichever is chosen, one name
   everywhere — `ui.md`, the API's `changes` parameter documentation, the
   Android and iOS strings.
2. **Whether the control carries a note.** Recommended: no. If yes, the line is
   `Trips stay within the chosen change limit.` in the services-note idiom.
3. **The accessible phrase.** `Up to 2 changes, on` / `Any number of changes,
   off`, matching `Trains, on`. c2's row instead relies on the existing
   "title, subtitle, then action" rule and adds no label.
4. **`ui.md`, journey line.** "The second change's alighting pin remains hidden
   on two-change results" becomes a rule about every change after the first, or
   is replaced by whichever treatment wins. The rule that names use separate
   lines when they intersect should also say what the row's height then is:
   145px untouched, 135px with the smaller band.
5. **`ui.md`, board.** "When six services are returned, all six remain whole
   and reachable at 390×844 and 412×732" cannot hold for a board of
   three-change rows. Either the sentence gains "at two changes", or the cap is
   what keeps it true and `Any` is documented as spending it.
6. **The Home smart header** needs the same rule as the board row, in writing:
   today it has none.

## 6 Recommendation

**Question 1 — c2, the row.** It is the only shape that leaves the Settings
screen on one page at the owner's own phone: 9px of scroll against 71px, 79px
and 83px. It adds no heading, no note and no new idiom — it is the Location row
the contract already specifies, one column shorter. Transplant from c1: if the
owner wants the choice to read as a family rather than a line, c1's band is the
right band and its cost is exactly 62px of scroll. Transplant from c4: its
specimen is the only thing on the sheet that explains what the choice does
without a word, and it would sit inside c2's row as a 60px line in place of the
empty icon column — that is the round-2 comp I would draw first. **It flips**
if the owner reads c2's mark as a statement rather than an action; that
ambiguity is the one thing a measurement cannot settle, and c1 has no such
ambiguity.

**Question 2 — c4's journey line.** It is the only treatment with no covered
numeral and no reversed pair at 390, at 412 and at font scale 1.3, and the only
one that keeps six whole services on a 390 board. Transplant from c2: its
travel-order slide is what makes c4 safe at enlarged text, and it should be
kept as the backstop rather than the main rule. Transplant from c3: c4 is c3
plus type, so a verdict for c3 is most of a verdict for c4. **It flips** if the
owner rules that a rider must always be told which platform they arrive on —
in which case the answer is c2's line, and the price is that a three-change
journey stops showing a time axis at all.

**Copy.** `Up to 2` / `Any number`, no note. Under the label `Changes` both
values finish the sentence the label starts; `2 max` and `No limit` read as
settings rather than answers, and they are the pair furthest from the owner's
own words when he ruled the value count.

## 7 Open questions for the owner

1. **Which Settings shape**, and is the 412×732 page worth 62–74px of scroll?
2. **Which words**, `Up to 2 / Any number` or `2 max / No limit`, and **does
   the control carry a note at all**?
3. **Where does it go**: with Services (c1, c2, c4) or last, after Appearance
   (c3)?
4. **Which journey line**, and behind it the real question: at three changes,
   **is the arrival platform of the middle changes a fact the row may drop**
   (c3, c4) or must every numeral stay even at the cost of the time axis (c2)?
5. **May a pin move off its true position on the axis?** c2 moves one by up to
   30px, c4 by up to 4px. `ui.md` treats the axis as measured truth and the
   probe pack has an axis-deviation check, so this is a rule change either way.
6. **Does the Home smart header follow the board's rule?** It has never hidden
   a numeral, and at three changes it is the worse of the two screens.
7. **Should `ui.md`'s "all six services whole at both frames" be qualified**,
   given a board of three-change rows shows four at 390 and three at 412?
8. **Is the three-change row worth designing at all**, or does the answer to a
   journey needing three changes belong on journey detail, with the board row
   saying only that there are three? Nothing in this round tests that; it is
   the alternative I did not draw because the brief froze the row's grammar.

## 8 What the next agent must know

- Reproduce every frame: `node tools/comps/shoot.js /tmp/trains-comps-transfer-cap-r1`
  from the repository root. Rebuild the sheet:
  `node tools/comps/sheet.js /tmp/trains-comps-transfer-cap-r1 && node /tmp/trains-comps-transfer-cap-r1/postprocess-sheet.js`.
  Re-measure: `node /tmp/trains-comps-transfer-cap-r1/measure.js`.
- **`postprocess-sheet.js` is not decoration.** `sheet.js` prints the board
  catalogue's own deltas (`delayed`, `cancelled`, `tight`, `D1`–`D8`) under
  "What is synthetic", and this round uses none of them — it drives the shipped
  renderer from `web/test/fixture.js` instead. Without the postprocess the
  owner is shown five synthetic declarations that are not true of these frames.
  It is idempotent.
- **`bundle-product.js` is the whole trick.** It rewrites nothing but `import`
  lines and the word `export`, wraps each module in an IIFE that returns its
  top-level names, and binds each module's imports from the previous ones.
  Unresolved modules (`storage`, `preferences`, `predict`, `search`,
  `stations`) bind to `{}`, which is safe only because nothing this round calls
  reaches them. If a later round needs `homeModel` or `boardModel`'s
  preference filtering, those modules must join the list. Regenerate after any
  change to the client: `node bundle-product.js`.
- **Scenario names.** Settings: `off` (flag off, the before), `settings`,
  `any`, `long-home`, `all-off`, `pairb` (c1's band with the other word pair),
  `settings-big`. Board: `two` (the exemplar), `three`, `three-long`,
  `three-all`, `three-big`, `two-big`. Home: `home`. Anything ending `-big`
  turns on the 1.3 text scale in `scale.js`.
- **Font scale on the web has no product mechanism**, because `web/app.css` is
  entirely px and has no `text-size-adjust`. `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 130%`
  under this harness's mobile emulation multiplies computed font sizes by 1.3
  and moves no box, which is the closest honest analogue of the Android font
  scale the product calibrates at. Verified in-page: 14px → 18.2px.
- **`assets/comps/latest/settings-390x844.png` is stale.** It says
  `Version 1.0.1`, `Choose nearby trips` and
  `ONLY TRIPS USING YOUR SELECTED SERVICES ARE SHOWN.`; the shipped renderer
  says `Version 1.2.4`, `Nearby trips use location` and
  `TRIPS USE CHOSEN SERVICES ONLY.` Those three differences in any comparison
  are the client's, already shipped, not a comp's design. The board exemplar
  is current: the round reproduces it pixel for pixel.
- **The B treatments are 30 lines of `cap-common.css` and three functions in
  `cap-common.js`** (`nudgePins`, `tightenNames`, `markMany`). They are scoped
  to `.cap-3`, a class this round puts on any row or header with three or more
  change names, so every two-change frame stays byte-identical to the exemplar.
  That gating is what makes the `two` scenario a valid regression check; do not
  remove it.
- **`belowFold` in `report.json` counts against the viewport, not the
  scroller**, so every board frame reports one. Read `scroller.extent` and the
  whole-item count beside it, as section 3 does.
- **Trap that cost a pass:** `journeyBarHtml` appends each change's pins in
  travel order but positions the alighting pin from the RIGHT edge of the dwell
  and the boarding pin from the LEFT, so on a short leg the later pin renders
  to the left of the earlier one AND paints over it. Sorting pins by their
  rendered position to fix crowding preserves the inversion; walk them in DOM
  order instead.
- `data.js` and `hdata.js` are the scaffold's generated catalogue tables. This
  round uses neither: it reads its journeys out of `web/test/fixture.js`
  through `product.js`. They are left in place because `new-round.js` only
  regenerates what an inherited workshop does not supply.
